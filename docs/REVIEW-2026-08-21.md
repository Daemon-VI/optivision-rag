## 1. E1's flat token-budget sweep is the thumbnail, not the corpus (Finding 2 is wrong)

ColSmol emits 875 tokens per page: 768 grid patches, a **64-token whole-page thumbnail**,
30 tile-marker tokens and 13 trailing text tokens. `keep_text_tokens: True` carries all
107 non-grid tokens verbatim through *every* pruning variant. The thumbnail is a low-res
encoding of the whole page, so "keep the top 10% of patches" is really "keep 77 grid
patches plus an unpruned global view". ColPali has no thumbnail (1024 + 7), which is why
E2 and E3 show a cliff and E1 does not.

`scripts/review/q4_sweep_ablation.py` re-runs the real pipeline from the cache with
subsets of the 107 tokens removed (nDCG@5 retention against that subset's own float
baseline; tok/pg after both pruning stages):

| variant | all (published) | grid + thumb | grid + instr/markers | grid only |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| binary-only | 87.9% | 90.6% | 87.0% | 91.0% |
| spatial+redundancy | 96.1% (247) | 97.0% (204) | 94.2% (183) | **91.1%** (140) |
| keep-50% binary | 85.7% (288) | 86.3% (245) | 82.2% (224) | 79.1% (181) |
| keep-30% binary | 85.9% (242) | 86.5% (199) | 82.6% (178) | 78.3% (135) |
| keep-20% binary | 85.3% (206) | 85.6% (163) | 80.3% (142) | 74.9% (99) |
| keep-10% binary | 85.6% (163) | 85.4% (120) | **66.3%** (99) | **60.0%** (56) |
| keep-5% binary | 79.9% (137) | 78.1% (94) | 33.6% (73) | 26.2% (30) |
| keep-10% float | 87.9% | 91.2% | 72.8% | 72.6% |

With the thumbnail the sweep is flat to 5%. Without it E1 falls off the same cliff as
docvqa (66.7% at keep-10%) and E3 (78.2%). The paper's explanation — "a corpus whose
salient content fits comfortably inside the top 10% of patches" — is contradicted by E3
on the identical corpus, and this table says why. Finding 1 moves too: grid-only
pruning costs 9% on E1, not 4%.

Consequences for the text: Sec. IV "875 vectors" should say what they are; Fig. 1's
"~247 vectors" are 43% unpruned tokens; Finding 2 and the "sparsity is cheap to measure"
implication need rewriting; the E1 rows of Tables I/V are measurements of *grid pruning
with a free global summary attached*.

Related: 52.3% of E1's MaxSim score comes from query tokens whose arg max is a non-grid
token, and the 30 tile markers (3.4% of the page) take 29.9% of all arg-max wins and
40.5% of the score (`q3_winners.py`). The first query token matches a non-grid token 98%
of the time. E1's ranking is half "structural tokens matching structural tokens".

## 2. "The encoder governs what the codec costs" is confounded by input resolution; VI-A's mechanism is wrong and so is the one the paper gestures at

Your own measurement already killed rho: 0.8001 (ColSmol) vs 0.7991 (ColPali) vs
0.7979 random. rho is a *per-vector* statistic and every roughly-Gaussian 128-d vector
has rho ≈ sqrt(2/pi) regardless of what the encoder learned. It cannot carry the claim.

What the E1→E3 swap also changes: ColSmol tiles the A4 page into 12 crops at 512 px and
can read an 11 pt code; ColPali squashes it to 448×448, where that code is ~5 px tall.
E3's ColPali is at the **subject-only floor**: if a retriever ranks by subject and cannot
read the code, the expected R@1 on this corpus is (60·0.2 + 12)/72 = 0.333 and nDCG@5 ≈
0.658. E3 measures 0.375 / 0.695. Its hit@5 = 1.000 everywhere and R@1 is *identical*
under float, int8 and binary — exactly what random reshuffling inside a 5-page subject
group looks like. nDCG@5 cannot see that reshuffling, so "loses nothing to one-bit codes"
is partly a floor effect.

The decisive local test (`q6_colsmol_notile.py`, 3 min on CPU): the same ColSmol, same
pages, same codec, image splitting **off** — one 512 px view, 79 tokens, as blind to the
code as ColPali.

| | float nDCG@5 / R@1 | precise-query R@1 | one-bit retention | R@1 under one-bit | tau over 60 pages |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ColSmol tiled (E1, 875 tok) | 0.7823 / 0.569 | 0.483 | **87.9%** | 0.417 | 0.643 |
| ColSmol untiled (79 tok) | 0.6376 / 0.333 | **0.217** | **97.3%** | 0.306 | 0.658 |
| ColPali 448 px (E3, 1031 tok) | 0.6954 / 0.375 | — | 98.4% | 0.375 | — |
| subject-only floor | 0.658 / 0.333 | 0.200 | | | |

Same encoder, same corpus: when it can read the code the one-bit codec costs 12.1
points; when it cannot, 2.7 — the same as ColPali. The codec's cost tracks **how
concentrated the discriminative evidence is** (one or two code patches vs. many
redundant subject/kind patches), not the encoder's patch geometry. E3 did not hold
"everything but the encoder" fixed; it also changed whether the discriminative evidence
was legible.

What *is* different about ColSmol's embedding distribution (and is measurable on any
cache, `q1_codec.py`): ||mean vector|| = 0.57 for unit vectors, mean pairwise cosine
0.33, 13 of 128 sign bits carry < 0.5 bit of entropy, covariance participation ratio
17.7 of 128. Blank-paper patches (80% of the grid) have pairwise cosine 0.42 — they are
near-duplicates whose sign codes differ by noise, and the max over ~600 of them per page
promotes distractors: the share of query tokens whose best grid match is a blank patch
goes from 34.7% (float) to 46.8% (sign). The untiled run has ||mu|| 0.40, 1 dead bit,
participation ratio 23.5 — so anisotropy and evidence granularity are entangled on this
corpus and I cannot fully separate them locally. But the float-side numbers (precise R@1
at the floor) are unambiguous about the resolution confound.

Codec ladder, E1, no pruning, 72 queries (`q1_codec.py`, `q5_codec_ext.py`):

| codec | bytes/vec | nDCG@5 retain | R@1 | tau(60) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| float32 | 512 | 100.0% | 0.569 | 1.000 |
| sign(d) — the pipeline | 16 | 87.9% | 0.417 | 0.643 |
| sign(d − mu), one global mean | 16 | 91.1% | 0.472 | 0.650 |
| sign(d − mu) · ‖d − mu‖ | 18 | 91.9% | 0.472 | 0.670 |
| sign(ITQ rotation (d − mu)) | 16 | 93.6–94.3% | 0.486 | 0.68 |
| 2-bit Lloyd-Max, random rotation | 32 | 94.5% | 0.486 | 0.790 |
| ColBERTv2-style: 4096 centroids + 1-bit residual | 17.5 | 92–100% (7 seeds, mean ≈97%) | 0.46–0.57 | 0.81 |
| int8 (repo) | 128 | 100.7% | 0.569 | 0.990 |

Paired bootstrap over queries: sign − float = −0.095 [−0.157, −0.035]; **ITQ − sign =
+0.045 [−0.032, +0.119], P(>0) = 0.88**. Not significant. The residual codec is better on
average but swings 8 points with the k-means seed, and with centroids fitted on pages
0–29 only it retains 72% — the centroids must cover the indexed pages. None of these is
a result at n = 72; they are a reason to run the ladder on the E2 caches (494 queries).
ITQ stacked on the real pipeline: `optivision` 86.7 → 94.9%, keep-50 85.7 → 91.6%,
keep-30 85.9 → 90.1%, keep-10 85.6 → 83.8%.

The honest statement for VI-A: E1 cannot resolve differences under ~8 nDCG points; the
one solid fact is that the raw sign codec loses ~10 points on tiled ColSmol and ~2–3 on
anything that retrieves by coarse evidence; and a sign code is the weakest one-bit codec
available — centring, rotation, or a centroid+residual code are free at the byte level
and are what ColBERTv2/PLAID actually use.

## 3. Stage-II: the headline is leakage, the proxy is already published, and E1 cannot adjudicate it

**"8.4% of patches ever win" counts the 107 structural tokens.** Grid-only it is 9.9%,
and 47.5% of all wins go to the 12% of tokens that are not grid patches.

**It is a coverage curve, not a property of the page.** Grid winners vs queries used: 1
query 1.2%, 8 → 5.3%, 36 → 9.2%, 72 → 9.9%, still rising; 1628 query tokens land on ~76
patches per page (21 tokens per winner) because the 72 queries share 12 subjects and the
same `Query:`/augmentation prefix. infovqa's 74.2% at 494 queries is a point on a
steeper curve. Report the curve and its extrapolation, not one point.

**The 100% held-out retention is subject leakage** (`q3_winners.py`, proper multi-relevant
nDCG@5, text tokens kept / grid only):

| split | winners kept | retention (text tokens kept) | retention (grid only) |
|---|---|---|---|
| random half (the ROADMAP number) | 9.1% | 100.0% | 100.0% |
| fit on 60 precise → test on 12 topical | 9.3% | 100.0% | 100.0% |
| fit on 12 topical → test on 60 precise | 6.5% | 88.7% | 85.3% |
| subject-disjoint, 3 draws | 6.1–7.2% | 98.5 / 98.9 / 92.4% | 96.0 / 91.7 / 84.7% |

Every held-out query in the random split has a same-subject twin in the fit half.

**`probe_eval.py` and `winner_stats.py` do not compute nDCG@5.** They credit only the
first hit with ideal DCG = 1, which is MRR-like and wrong for the 12 topical queries
(up to 5 relevant pages each). That is why they print 0.8016 for the full index where
`bench` prints 0.7823. Use `metrics.evaluate`.

**Random probes ≈ fitted probes, and more probes beat better-placed probes.** Your own
control already says this on infovqa (cb-random ≥ cb-farthest at 2 of 3 budgets). Locally,
256 random probes vs 4096 random probes vs 256 farthest, selection alone, held-out half,
float:

| keep 30% (230 patches) | + text tokens | grid only | | keep 10% (77) | + text | grid only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| full grid | 0.749 | 0.794 | | | 0.749 | 0.794 |
| pixel | 0.748 | 0.815 | | pixel | 0.717 | 0.567 |
| probe: 256 farthest | 0.736 | 0.781 | | 256 farthest | 0.686 | 0.705 |
| probe: 256 random | 0.738 | 0.762 | | 256 random | 0.669 | 0.659 |
| **probe: 4096 random** | **0.749** | **0.799** | | 4096 random | 0.687 | **0.759** |
| uniform random subset | 0.691 | 0.618 | | uniform random | 0.663 | 0.508 |
| pool: agglomerative → k | 0.719 | 0.769 | | **pool: agglomerative → k** | **0.742** | **0.762** |

The win count is a Monte-Carlo estimate of the measure of each patch's normal cone (the
set of query directions for which it is the arg max). 256 samples over 1000 patches is
mostly zeros plus the tie-break; the "coverage vs fit" story is sample size. With 4096
directions the estimate is good and the probe placement stops mattering. Farthest-point
is not the active ingredient. This construction — Voronoi/normal-cone measure estimated
by random directions — is the published method of *A Voronoi Cell Formulation for
Principled Token Pruning in Late-Interaction Retrieval Models* (SIGIR 2026,
arXiv 2603.09933), and the exact-dominance version is *Towards Lossless Token Pruning in
Late-Interaction Retrieval Models* (Zong & Piwowarski, SIGIR 2025).

**The budget comparison on infovqa is not token-matched.** At keep-10% pixel stores 85.2
tok/pg and cb-keep stores 102.8 (20% more), because the redundancy stage collapses
spatially contiguous pixel selections harder. Interpolating pixel to the codebook's token
count: farthest +1.6 / +3.1 / +3.2 at 50/30/10%, random +2.0 / +2.9 / +4.3. Smaller,
still positive, random still ≥ farthest. The control that is missing is **uniform random
patch selection at the matched budget**: at keep-50% half of cb-random's selection is
already tie-break noise and it scores 96.7%, the best row. My expectation is that uniform
random at 50% on infovqa is ≈ 95–96%, i.e. the finding is "pixel saliency is harmful on
dense pages", not "probes are good".

**Why pixel saliency is harmful on infographics:** ink = (95th-percentile − gray) assumes
dark ink on light paper. Invert an E1 page and the ink term selects 0% ink cells at
keep-10% (the edge term, which is polarity-invariant, selects 100%). Light text on dark
panels is the infovqa norm. Edge-only or |gray − local median| is the one-line fix and
would change the E2 pixel rows.

**Prior work that decides the direction.** Ma et al., *Towards Storage-Efficient Visual
Document Retrieval* (ACL Findings 2025, arXiv 2506.04997) tested pruning vs merging on
ColPali/ColQwen2 and found *random pruning beats every sophisticated pruning method* and
that merging (Light-ColPali) keeps 98.2% at 11.8% memory, 94.6% at 2.8%. DocPruner
(arXiv 2509.23883) reaches the opposite conclusion with [EOS]-attention pruning at an
adaptive per-document threshold (~50–60% reduction, near-lossless on ViDoRe-v2). The
redundancy stage is Clavié et al., *Token Pooling* (arXiv 2409.14683). HPC-ColPali
(arXiv 2506.21601) is a k-means-quantize + attention-prune + binary pipeline. None is
cited. The paper's "individual components are not novel" needs these names, and Stage-II
as a *contribution* (a query-free selector) is already occupied on both the theory and
the empirical side; the E1 evidence for it is within noise.

**Bit allocation:** on ColPali the entire 1-bit → 8-bit range spans 2.6 (infovqa) to 3.7
(docvqa) points, so allocating bits by predicted win rate can recover at most that, and a
uniform 2-bit code (16×) will take most of it. On ColSmol the range is 12 points, but
the winner set is only ~85–96% predictable out of subject. Predicted-win-rate allocation
is not where the gain is; fixing the one-bit codec (centre/rotate/residual) and adding a
2-bit row is. If you want non-uniform precision, the principled version is the
ColBERTv2 residual codec, not per-patch bit tiers.

## 4. Kendall tau: what to report

Same pipeline, E1, against the float baseline's full ranking (`q2_rank_agreement.py`):

| variant | retain | tau(60) | tau shared-top10 (paper) | tau_AP | RBO p=.9 | top-1 same | top-5 overlap | gold@1 kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| int8-only | 100.7% | 0.990 | 0.972 | 0.985 | 0.986 | 0.97 | 0.99 | 1.00 |
| spatial+redundancy | 96.1% | 0.887 | 0.866 | 0.870 | 0.911 | 0.79 | 0.97 | 0.83 |
| binary-only | 87.9% | 0.643 | 0.585 | 0.629 | 0.758 | **0.50** | **0.93** | 0.68 |
| optivision | 86.7% | 0.624 | 0.606 | 0.612 | 0.751 | 0.47 | 0.96 | 0.63 |
| keep-10% | 85.6% | 0.578 | 0.551 | 0.566 | 0.718 | 0.36 | 0.92 | 0.61 |

Chance levels: tau and tau_AP ≈ 0 at any N; RBO(.9) = 0.167 at N=60 but 0.020 at N=500;
top-5 overlap 0.08 vs 0.01.

- Your fix (tau-b over the corpus, absentees tied last) is a correct Kendall tau for full
  rankings and the absentee rule is the sensible case of Fagin et al.'s top-k extension.
  But it weights a swap at ranks 400/401 the same as 1/2, so on a 500-page corpus it is
  mostly a statement about the tail. It is fine *within* an experiment and should never
  be compared across corpora — no statistic repairs that, because the two tasks differ.
- The paper's actual argument ("shortlist for a reader vs ranked list for a person") is
  made directly and without ambiguity by **top-1 agreement** and **top-k set overlap** with
  the float baseline: binary keeps 93% of the float top-5 set but agrees on the top-1 only
  50% of the time. Those two numbers *are* the finding; report them in every table.
- If one correlation is wanted, use **tau_AP** (Yilmaz, Aslam & Robertson, SIGIR 2008):
  top-weighted, a correlation (chance 0, corpus-size-stable), and asymmetric with the
  float ranking as the reference, which is the right asymmetry here. RBO (Webber, Moffat &
  Zobel, TOIS 2010) is the standard for *indefinite* lists, but you have full rankings,
  and its chance level depends on N, which reintroduces the comparability problem.
- Table I/II/V still quote the shared-top-10 statistic; the Metrics section says
  "against the baseline's own ranking". Either regenerate the tables (the bench now records
  both) or say in the caption which one is shown.

## 5. Smaller things a reviewer will find

- **Compression ratios are against float32.** ColPali ran in bfloat16 and every
  deployment stores fp16/bf16, so 53–60× is 27–30× against the index people actually
  keep. State the baseline precision in the tables.
- **`min_keep` is 8 in E1 and 16 in E2/E3** (configs); the paper says 8. Negligible
  effect, but say it.
- **ROADMAP item 2** says `benchmark.json` holds per-query runs. It does not (`rows` only).
  Per-family E3 numbers — the cheapest test of §2 — need the runs saved; add them to the
  report or dump `deep` per variant.
- `reports/winner_stats_infovqa_test_subsampled.txt` still shows the `KeyError: 'spec'`
  traceback from before the fix; it should be regenerated or the ROADMAP should say the
  infovqa held-out half never ran.
- Sec. VI-A's first paragraph says sign codes are safe because distortion is uniform and
  then blames the arg max. Tau per query tracks the per-query score-gap-to-noise ratio
  (corr 0.79 across E1's queries, `q1_codec.py`); the "arg max flip rate" is the wrong
  statistic because flips among near-duplicate patches are free — which is why ColPali
  flips more (76.6%) and loses less.
- E3 in Table VI ("134.5× at 98.7%") is the best cell of the 2×2 and is a retriever at
  the subject floor; the text already hedges it, but after §2 it should not be in the
  operating-point table at all.

## 6. Experiments, where they run, what each outcome means

Local, CPU, minutes:

1. **Big-font corpus with tiled ColSmol** (add a font-scale knob to `make-corpus`, render
   the code at ~3× so it survives 448 px; re-encode with ColSmol tiled, ~30 min). This is
   the clean version of `q6`: same 875 tokens, same blank fraction, only the legibility of
   the code changes. *If* the one-bit loss stays ~12 points, concentration of evidence is
   not the mechanism and the blank-patch noise story is; if it drops, §2 is confirmed
   from the other side. Then run the same corpus through E3 on Kaggle: if ColPali's loss
   *rises* once it can read the code, the double dissociation's encoder axis is dead.
2. **Regenerate the E1 sweep with `keep_text_tokens: False`** (or a `keep_thumbnail` flag)
   — `q4` already did this; it just needs to become a reported row.
3. **Uniform-random selection and agglomerative pooling rows** in `codebook_sweep` — the
   two controls Stage-II lacks. Local first (`q3` has them), then the infovqa run.
4. **Per-family (precise/topical) metrics in every E1/E3 report**, and paired bootstrap
   CIs on every headline difference. At n=72 anything under ~8 points is noise.
5. **Polarity-robust saliency** (edge-only or |gray − local median|) as a variant.

Kaggle, one run each:

6. **E3 with `KEEP_CACHE=1`** — the cache is 60 × 1031 × 128 × 4 B ≈ 30 MB, not 1 GB.
   Then run `q1_codec.py` on it: ||mu||, dead bits, participation ratio, blank-patch
   cosine, distractor-promotion rate, and the codec ladder. This is the controlled
   geometry comparison the ROADMAP asks for, and it costs minutes.
7. **Codec ladder on the E2 caches** (494/451 queries): sign vs centred vs ITQ vs 2-bit vs
   centroid+residual (K ≈ 16·√N). If ITQ/2-bit close ColPali's remaining 2.6–3.7 points,
   "the codec is nearly free" becomes "the codec is free", and the Stage-II bit-allocation
   item is closed before it opens.
8. **infovqa/docvqa with the random and pooling controls and the polarity-fixed pixel
   detector**, token-matched. This decides whether there is any selection result to write
   up. My prediction: uniform random ≈ probes ≥ pixel at 50%; pooling ≥ all at 10%.

## 7. What I would make the paper say

The corpus-axis half of the dissociation is solid and interesting: pruning yield is a
property of page sparsity, and with the thumbnail removed E1 even agrees with E2 on the
cliff. The encoder-axis half should become: *a raw sign codec costs ~10–12 points when
the ranking hinges on one or two fine-grained patch matches and ~2–3 when it hinges on
many redundant ones; which regime a retriever is in depends on input resolution and on
the evidence granularity of the corpus, not on the encoder's patch geometry; and the sign
code is the worst one-bit code available, with centring, rotation and centroid-residual
codes recovering most of the loss at zero byte cost.* That is a more defensible and more
useful paper than the current VI-A, and every piece of it is measurable from caches the
runner already produces.
